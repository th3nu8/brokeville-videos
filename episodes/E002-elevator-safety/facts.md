# Facts used (E002 how an elevator stops itself from falling)
- A traction elevator car rides on guide rails along the shaft; the rails keep it from swaying. The counterweight also rides on its own rails. https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/engines-equipment/elevator3.htm
- The car hangs from several steel ropes, typically between four and eight, and one rope can support the car and counterweight on its own. https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/engines-equipment/elevator4.htm
- The ropes loop over a grooved wheel (sheave) turned by an electric motor in a machine room above the shaft; the counterweight weighs about the same as the car filled to 40 percent of capacity. https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/engines-equipment/elevator3.htm
- The machine brake is a pair of friction shoes applied by springs and lifted electrically; if power is lost, the brake applies. https://www.otis.com/tools-resources/high-rise-safety-systems
- The overspeed governor has its own pulley moved by a steel rope (the governor rope) attached to the car; if the car goes too fast, flyweights spring outward and trip a device that grabs the governor rope, which pulls on the car's safeties. https://www.otis.com/tools-resources/high-rise-safety-systems
- The safeties are a mechanical brake under the cab that wedges itself onto the rail when overspeed is detected. https://tkelevator.com/us-en/tools/classroom-on-demand/emergency-brake.html
- Buffers at the bottom of the hoistway absorb the energy of a descending car, like giant automotive shock absorbers (hydraulic or gas-spring types). https://www.otis.com/tools-resources/high-rise-safety-systems , https://www.explainthatstuff.com/how-elevators-work.html
- Elisha Otis publicly demonstrated his safety elevator in New York in 1854 (Crystal Palace / Exhibition of the Industry of All Nations); he stood on the platform and had the ropes cut, and it did not fall. Original device: a spring that snapped into notches/teeth in the rails. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisha_Otis , https://tkelevator.com/us-en/tools/classroom-on-demand/emergency-brake.html
